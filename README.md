What is WikiHow2Network?
Computer networking has a bad habit of sounding like an ancient language spoken exclusively by sysadmins. WikiHow2Network is my attempt to fix that. It’s a clean, beginner-friendly web app designed to explain how the internet actually works under the hood and without drowning you in dry jargon or 800-page textbooks.

Whether you're trying to figure out what a subnet mask does or why your router hates you today, the goal here is simple: fast, digestible answers that make sense.

Key Features
8 Bite-Sized Lessons: Core topics broken down for humans, covering topics such as IP addresses, DNS, the OSI model, routers, switches, firewalls, ports, and subnetting.

Search & Category Filters: Quickly jump to what you need instead of digging through walls of text.

Progress Tracking: Saves your completed lessons locally in your browser so you can pick up where you left off.

Networking Glossary: A searchable quick-reference guide for all those 3-letter acronyms.

Interactive Quiz: Test your knowledge with immediate feedback (so you know why an answer was right or wrong).

Interactive Network Lab: A live, client-side simulation showing how a DNS lookup works step-by-step. It runs entirely in your browser—no external API calls or backend magic required.

Responsive Design: Fits comfortably on desktop, tablet, or phone screens.

Tech Stack

HTML: Pure structure and semantics.

CSS: Custom styles and responsive layouts (no heavy frameworks bloat).

JavaScript: Powers lesson data, dynamic searching/filtering, quiz logic, the DNS simulation, and progress tracking.

Browser local Storage: Keeps track of completed lessons without needing a database.
